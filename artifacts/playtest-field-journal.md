## My discoveries

### The compost hub — West Oakland Farm Park

Oakland’s curbside organic material can become finished compost. Farm Park offers a real pickup resource.

My explanation: Compost can help care for soil, but growing still needs water, time, and upkeep.

### Living soil — West Oakland Farm Park

In the same rain experiment, 68 of 100 game drops soaked into my bed, compared with 36 in the uncovered reference.

My explanation: I changed soil cover or organic matter, not the rain. Real infiltration also depends on soil, slope, rainfall, and maintenance.

### A growing bed — West Oakland Farm Park

Under the same later-season dry period, A used 6 water tokens and B used 4. Moisture indicators were 62 and 50.

My explanation: The drip approach used two fewer water tokens, but used more credits and gave up the compost benefit.

### The big trees — deFremery Park

I explored a young tree at year five, with 2 care tokens. Shade was 58/100 in the learning model.

My explanation: A young tree needs time and continuing establishment care before its canopy can shade a gathering place.

### A shady spot — deFremery Park

I moved the sun and tested bench 1. It was in sunshine at my chosen position in the illustration.

My explanation: A tree’s shade moves. The canopy, the place we sit, and the time all matter; being near a tree does not guarantee shade all day.

### A shared space — deFremery Park

I compared two hypothetical gathering ideas at year 5. A used 6 credits; B used 6, with gathering indicators of 70 and 48.

My explanation: A built canopy gives shade sooner, but uses the whole budget and leaves no credits for seating. A cared-for tree develops habitat over time.

My explanation: I noticed that caring for existing soil and trees can change how land supports people. Time, water, and maintenance limit every new idea.

## Real places and possible next steps

### West Oakland Farm Park
2847 Peralta Street
Acta Non Verba describes gardens, growing spaces, picnic areas, play structures, and a compost hub at this community farm park. The city launched its free compost pickup hub here in May 2026.
Explore ANV volunteering: https://anvfarm.org/get-involved/volunteer-with-us/
Source checked October 24, 2026: https://anvfarm.org/our-farms/west-oakland-farm-park/

### deFremery Park
1651 Adeline Street
Oakland documents mature trees, green fields, picnic areas, recreation facilities, and community gathering at deFremery Park. The neighborhood also knows it as Little Bobby Hutton Park.
Read park & recreation information: https://www.oaklandca.gov/Community/Parks-Facilities/Recreation-Centers/DeFremery-Recreation-Center
Source checked October 24, 2026: https://www.oaklandca.gov/Community/Parks-Facilities/Recreation-Centers/DeFremery-Recreation-Center

### West Oakland Library
1801 Adeline Street
The West Oakland Branch offers collections for all ages, computers, and meeting and study rooms. Our field-journal desk is a fictional learning scene, rather than an advertised library program.
Explore the West Oakland Branch: https://oaklandlibrary.org/locations/was/
Source checked October 24, 2026: https://oaklandlibrary.org/locations/was/

Schedules, access, age eligibility, and availability must be confirmed with each provider. Source checks are not steward approval or a partnership.

Oakland planning information: https://www.oaklandca.gov/Government/Boards-Commissions/Planning-Commission
Before proposing a real change, identify a willing steward and an appropriate planning process/recipient. Ask about ownership, soil safety, drainage, accessibility, water, costs, and long-term care.
